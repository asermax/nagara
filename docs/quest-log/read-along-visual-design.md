# Read-along visual design

## Destination

A proper visual design for the player and, by extension, the web surface: a design language, typography, colour, motion, and light and dark, coherent enough that a stranger reads it as a finished product. Judged on aesthetic coherence and quality against real content, once a direction exists to judge.

## Bearings

**The ground.** How the player and the web surface look. Not how they are laid out or navigated: [[read-along-player-shape]] settled the shape, and that shape is the fixed substrate this must not disturb.

**Read first.** [[read-along-player-shape]] for what is already settled and therefore not up for redesign; [[read-along-player]] for the surface itself; [[listening-experience]] for what the product is trying to feel like.

**Standing preferences.** The placeholder aesthetic the shape was validated against **carried no verdict weight** and is not a starting point; treat it as absent rather than as a first draft. Judge against real articles rather than sample text, because the content is long-form prose and that is exactly where typography either works or does not.

## Trials

- **What the design direction is.** Nothing else here can be phrased until there is one, which is what makes this an adventure rather than a session of work: a direction is not a question you answer, it is a thing you produce and then react to.
- **Whether a direction survives real content**, judged on coherence and quality against real articles rather than on taste in isolation.

Both stay coarse on purpose. The concrete pieces, typography, colour, motion, light and dark, only become separately answerable once a direction exists to derive them from.

## Solved

Nothing yet.

## Out of scope

- **The player's layout, navigation and structure.** Settled by [[read-along-player-shape]], including the calm-reader and focus-mode split. If a visual direction seems to require changing it, that is a finding worth recording, not a licence.
